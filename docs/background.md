## Theses

**The use of multilayer networks for the molecular characterization of
hepatoblastoma** — MSc thesis, 2022

Hepatoblastoma (HB) is an uncommon liver cancer frequently diagnosed in
children under 5, with a largely unknown origin. This work characterized the
current landscape of high-throughput transcriptomics data in HB through a
meta-analysis of 5 publicly available RNA-Seq studies (181 samples, 101
patients), identifying commonly dysregulated genes. A multilayer network
spanning metabolic reactions, protein-protein interactions in the liver, and
pathways was built to uncover key genes driving the spread of dysregulation —
surfacing four candidate genes (`LRRC52`, `DLX6`, `DLX5`, `MSX2`) linked to
the β-catenin pathway implicated in HB. The workflow was deployed as a
code-free tool to the Virtual Research Environment of the EU H2020 project
iPC (ref. 826121).

[Read the thesis (PDF)](files/msc-thesis.pdf)

**Design and optimization of primers for gene transcript and methylation
analysis on bovine embryos** — BSc thesis, 2018

Work carried out as part of an ongoing Ghent University project on sperm
stressors and their effect on sperm methylation and subsequent embryo
development in cattle. Designed, tested, and optimized primers for embryo
transcript expression analysis and for bisulfite-treated embryos, validating
specificity and efficiency via RT-qPCR, melt curve analysis, agarose gel
electrophoresis, and Sanger sequencing.

[Read the thesis](http://hdl.handle.net/10256/16406)
